Tide charts: bar charts everyone can read

The Haneul design system had beautiful charts that screen readers skipped entirely and keyboards couldn't reach. I rebuilt them so every value can be found by mouse, keyboard or voice.

Try it: hover a bar, or press Tab to reach the chart and the arrow keys to move between days.

role
Design systems engineer
team
2 engineers, 1 designer
timeline
One quarter
stack
Web components, SVG

the problem

Haneul’s charts were drawn as a single image. They looked right, but to a screen reader the whole chart was one word: “graphic.” Keyboard users couldn’t reach a single value, and the colours only just told the series apart.

Teams worked around it by pasting a table under every chart, which meant two things to keep in sync and pages twice as long.

what I built

  • One tab stop per chart. Tab reaches the chart once, then the arrow keys move between bars, and Home and End jump to the first and last. Fifty bars don’t mean fifty tab presses.
  • Every bar has a name. Each one is announced as its label and value, like “Thu, 9.2 m,” and the chart says what it shows and how to move through it.
  • Values on hover, focus and tap. The same label appears whichever way you got there, so nobody needs a mouse to read the numbers.
  • Contrast that passes. Bars meet 3:1 against the background in light and dark mode, and series differ in shade as well as hue.

Teams use it with one line: <tide-bar-chart>, passing the data in. Accessibility comes built in, so nobody has to remember it.
