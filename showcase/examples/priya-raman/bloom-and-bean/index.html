A neighbourhood café’s new look

Bloom & Bean had great coffee and a loyal crowd, but a logo made in a free app and a menu nobody could read from the queue.

Bloom & Bean
Client
Bloom & Bean, Leeds (fictional)
What I did
Identity, menus, website
Timeline
7 weeks
Result
Online orders doubled

The brief

Sam and Jo opened Bloom & Bean in 2021. Four years on, they wanted a look that felt as warm as the café itself, and a website people could order from without phoning.

What I made

I spent two mornings in the café watching how people ordered. Most decided in the queue, squinting at a chalkboard. So the menu came first: big, simple, and grouped the way people actually ask for things.

The identity grew out of the menu: a sunny yellow from the café’s front door, a friendly rounded typeface, and a small set of colours for labels and cups.

Last came the website: one page with opening hours, the menu, and an order button that sends people to their existing ordering service, so there was nothing new for the team to learn.

What changed

2×online orders in the first three months
40sshorter average wait in the queue
0monthly fees for the website

“Customers keep telling us the new menus are the first thing they notice,” Sam says. The numbers are made up for this example, but this is the kind of result a real case study would show.
