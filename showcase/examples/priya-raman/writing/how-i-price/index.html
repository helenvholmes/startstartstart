How I price brand projects, and why I share it

Most designers say “get in touch for a quote.” I put my prices on my website instead. Here’s how I work them out.

When I started freelancing, I hid my prices. I thought showing them would scare people off, or that I’d lose work I could have won by quoting lower. What actually happened was a lot of friendly first calls with people whose budget was a tenth of what the work cost. Nobody enjoyed those calls.

So two years ago I started publishing a starting price for each kind of project. Fewer people get in touch now, but almost everyone who does is a good fit.

Where the numbers come from

Every price starts from the same three things:

  1. How many days it really takes. I track my time on every project, so I know a small brand identity takes about 14 working days, including the parts clients don’t see, like research and file prep.
  2. My day rate. It covers my time, software, insurance, pension and the weeks between projects. It isn’t what I’d like to earn, it’s what keeps the business running.
  3. A margin for the unexpected. Around 15%, because something always comes up: an extra round of feedback, a printer who needs a different file.

“From” means that’s the price for the usual version of the project. If yours is bigger, I’ll tell you how much more and why before we start, never halfway through.

Why sharing it helps you

  • You can tell straight away whether we’re a fit, without booking a call.
  • You can plan your budget, or save up for the bit you need most first.
  • You’re paying the same as everyone else for the same work.

If the full project is out of reach, tell me anyway. Sometimes we can start with the logo and a one-page site, and add the rest when you’re ready.
